## 1. Site Map Questions

**Museum websites researched**
- The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA): https://www.moma.org
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art: https://www.metmuseum.org
- Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum: https://www.guggenheim.org

**What pages do the sites have in common?**
All three have a Visit / Plan Your Visit page (hours, tickets, directions, accessibility), Exhibitions (current and upcoming), an online Collection you can search, Events / Calendar, Membership, a Shop, Support / Donate, and an About section.

**What pages are different or unique?**
- The Met has pages for its second location (The Met Cloisters) and a large research section with art history essays and a timeline.
- MoMA has pages for its sister location, MoMA PS1, plus a Magazine and online courses.
- The Guggenheim has pages for its global network of museums (Bilbao, Venice, Abu Dhabi) and a section about the Frank Lloyd Wright building itself.

**What is good about these sites, and what don't you like?**
Good: big, high-quality images; ticket buying is easy to find from every page; the main navigation is short and consistent. Not so good: the menus get very deep, so it can take several clicks to find simple things like parking; the Met's site has so much content that searching is sometimes easier than browsing; some pages are heavy and load slowly.

**Site map – 263 Modern Art**

```
263 Modern Art – Site Map

Home
├── Visit            (hours, tickets, directions, accessibility)
├── Exhibitions      (current, upcoming, past)
├── Collection       (search the permanent collection)
├── Artists          (featured and local artists)
├── Events           (calendar, talks, gallery nights)
├── Education        (classes, school groups, tours)
├── Membership       (levels and benefits)
├── Shop             (prints, books, gifts)
└── About & Contact  (mission, staff, press, contact form)
```

**Rationale**
263 Modern Art is a small gallery, so the site focuses on getting people in the door and keeping them coming back. Visit and Exhibitions are the pages visitors need most, so they come first. Because modern art can feel intimidating, the Artists and Education pages explain the work and the people behind it and give schools and new visitors a way in. Events and Membership give repeat visitors a reason to return and help fund the gallery, and the Shop adds revenue. About & Contact is combined into one page because a small gallery doesn't need separate pages for each.
